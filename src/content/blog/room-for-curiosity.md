It is easy to turn curiosity into another task. Read this book. Finish that course. Build something from every interesting idea.

But curiosity can also be lighter than that. A question can be worth asking even if it does not become a project.

## Notice before you measure

An unusual sentence, a well-designed door handle, or a surprising behavior in a program can all be invitations to pay attention.

The first step is not to decide whether the observation is valuable. The first step is to notice it.

Keep a place for these little things. A few words are enough:

- Why does this interaction feel so natural?
- What makes this explanation easy to follow?
- Is there a simpler way to do this?

## Leave some questions open

Not every question needs an immediate answer. Sometimes a better question appears after you have lived with the first one for a while.

An open question is not a failure to finish. It is a place to come back to.

## Make space, not a schedule

Try a small pause between finishing one thing and starting another. Look away from the screen. Take a walk without turning it into a planning meeting.

The point is not to optimize the pause. It is to leave a little room for something unexpected.

You might find a new idea. You might just enjoy the afternoon. Either is enough.
